@IMAGE 0
Challenger Astronauts Memorialized on the Moon
Apollo is a 524 km-wide impact basin located inside the giant South Pole–Aitken basin on the Moon. NASA identified it as a potential region for future human lunar exploration. After the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster, seven craters on Apollo Basin's eastern rim were named in honor of the seven crew members who lost their lives.

@IMAGE 1
Apollo 8’s Iconic Earthrise
The Apollo 8 crew captured a rising Earth about five degrees above the Moon’s horizon near 110° east longitude. Taken on December 24 at 15:40 GMT, the image shows Earth's sunset line crossing Africa, the South Pole near its left end, and clouds covering North and South America.

@IMAGE 2
A New View of the Moon
On April 6, 2026, the Artemis II crew captured Earth setting behind the Moon’s curved edge. The image shows the Orientale and Hertzsprung impact basins, Vavilov crater, and secondary crater chains. The dark side of Earth is experiencing nighttime, while clouds are visible over Australia and Oceania.

@IMAGE 3
Faces of Shackleton
Shackleton Crater is a 21 km-wide crater at the Moon's south pole, where most of its interior remains permanently in shadow. The image shows four views of the crater: its topography from LOLA, visible lighting conditions from SMART-1, illumination patterns from the LRO Camera, and radar data from Mini-RF overlaid on a shaded relief map.
